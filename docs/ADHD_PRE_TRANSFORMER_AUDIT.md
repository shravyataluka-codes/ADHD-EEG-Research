# ADHD EEG Research: Pre-Transformer Readiness and Architecture Decision Audit

**Scope:** Read-only repository and literature assessment. No files were modified, no Transformer code was written, no model was trained, and no real-data splits were generated.

## 1. Executive readiness verdict

**The project is not ready for a scientifically valid Transformer experiment.** Data access and synthetic-tested utilities exist, but the mapping from EEG trials to participants and clinical labels is not independently verified. The participant-aware protocol is not frozen, and the preprocessing implementation conflicts with the Sprint 2 contract, which does not approve signal transformations.

**Engineering readiness:** The repository can read bounded HDF5 trials in the documented tensor shape, preserve source files, and exercise a generic group splitter on synthetic inputs. Preprocessing code also runs against synthetic data.

**Scientific readiness:** Insufficient for supervised training or participant-independent evaluation on this cohort. Candidate identities and group labels cannot establish these claims, and the current code cannot make them authoritative.

There is also a **research-direction conflict** to resolve before architecture work: the latest [Research Decision and Implementation Plan](docs/RESEARCH_DECISION_AND_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) describes a reproducibility benchmark whose primary model is EEGNet and explicitly says not to implement a Transformer. The [master guide](adhd-eeg-transformer-master-guide.md) proposes a hybrid Conformer, while the [ML execution plan](docs/ML_EXECUTION_PLAN.md) puts Transformer justification and implementation later, after earlier gates. The user’s current Transformer objective therefore needs to be reconciled with the repository’s latest research decision.

The repository does not contain one unambiguous “eight-sprint” roadmap: [PROJECT-SPRINT-MAP.md](docs/PROJECT-SPRINT-MAP.md) describes Phases A–G, while [ML_EXECUTION_PLAN.md](docs/ML_EXECUTION_PLAN.md) enumerates Sprints 0–9. I use the latter below when referencing its specific gates; this is a documentation inconsistency, not a reason to restart completed work.

**Bottom line:** A synthetic encoder could be prototyped in isolation, but that would not complete a project gate or establish scientific readiness. Under the repository’s plan, resolve the research-direction conflict, data/label eligibility, protocol, and baseline gates before treating a Transformer implementation as project work.

## 5. Unresolved scientific limitations

1. **Identity and target validity:** The all-trial EEG-to-metadata join, row-run-to-person link, and identity-keyed diagnosis record are absent from the inspected evidence. No classifier result can repair an invalid or unknown target.
2. **Representation comparability:** The paper-reported 256 Hz and nominal 1.5 s do not explain 385 stored positions. Prior cohort papers also differ in reported sampling and preprocessing descriptions. Faithful reproduction requires resolving what the supplied tensor represents; a limited study can preserve indices without physical-time claims.
3. **Channel and signal semantics:** The 56-to-60 mapping, channel order, units/reference, and local upstream cleaning state remain unknown. This blocks spatial/physiological interpretation and decisions that depend on physical amplitude.
4. **Preprocessing conflict:** Code executes filter/notch/ICA, but Gate 2 defers these operations. The preprocessing CLI must not be used for a research experiment until the protocol is approved and its split interaction is specified.
5. **Statistical unit:** 33,902 trials are repeated measurements from a reported 144 children, not 33,902 independent people. Participant-independent claims need verified IDs and a participant-level target.
6. **Contribution:** A Transformer on this cohort is already published by He et al.; subject-held-out studies and random-trial/LOSO contrasts are also in prior work. Novelty must be a clear, evidence-backed question, not “new architecture” or an unverified claim that prior work leaked.

## 6. Three-architecture literature comparison

### Published evidence

- **He et al. (2023), EEG-Transformer:** [Primary paper, DOI 10.1088/1741-2552/acf7f5](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/acf7f5). Uses the same Dresden/OSF cohort. Repository analysis describes a learnable positional embedding, six encoder blocks and six heads, global max pooling, and a three-class head. Paper-reported results differ between abstract and main text (95.85% vs. 95.58% ± 0.52%); parameter and sample-size statements also conflict. Its described five-fold split is trial-level; participant independence is **not established**, which is not proof of leakage. [Repository analysis](docs/base-paper-analysis.md), [literature review](docs/literature-review/ADHD_EEG_Literature_Review.md).
- **Wang et al. (2022), CNN-LSTM:** [Primary paper, DOI 10.1088/1741-2552/ac7f5d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/ac7f5d). Same cohort and `(385, 56)`-style ERP input description. Reports 98.23% under random trial five-fold evaluation and 39.59% ± 28.11% under leave-one-subject-out. This is direct evidence that split protocol materially changes the reported estimate, not evidence that a specific architecture wins.
- **Vahid et al. (2019), EEGNet:** [Primary paper, DOI 10.3390/jcm8071055](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm8071055). Same 144-child cohort; EEGNet evaluated leave-one-child-out. The literature review reports 39% ± 30% whole-cohort mean three-class accuracy and substantially higher results for a selected above-chance subset. The selected-subset figure must not be used as population-wide performance. [Repository review](docs/literature-review/ADHD_EEG_Literature_Review.md).
- **Song et al. (2023), EEG Conformer:** [Primary paper, DOI 10.1109/TNSRE.2022.3230250](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNSRE.2022.3230250). Proposes convolutional feature extraction with self-attention and evaluates on three public EEG decoding datasets across motor-imagery and emotion-recognition paradigms. Its abstract claims state-of-the-art performance and released code. It is not evidence for ADHD performance or participant-independent evaluation on this cohort; the accessible abstract does not establish a directly comparable split protocol.
- **Lawhern et al. (2018), EEGNet:** [Primary paper, DOI 10.1088/1741-2552/aace8c](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/aace8c). Proposes compact depthwise/separable convolution and reports within- and cross-subject experiments across P300, ERN, MRCP, and SMR BCI paradigms. It motivates a compact baseline, not a claim that EEGNet is best for this diagnosis task.
- **Vaswani et al. (2017), original Transformer:** [“Attention Is All You Need,” arXiv:1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762). The original work is machine translation, not EEG. Its attention mechanism does not itself justify raw EEG tokenization or a clinical/physiological interpretation.

### Decision matrix

The complexity comparison below is structural, not a benchmark. For 385 time tokens, full attention forms $385^2 = 148{,}225$ token-pair scores per head per block. A hypothetical convolutional tokenizer with stride 8 yields about 49 tokens and $49^2 = 2{,}401$ scores, roughly 62 times fewer attention-pair positions. Exact parameter counts cannot be compared until depth, width, FFN, convolution kernels, and pooling are specified. The repository’s own He-paper analysis notes conflicting parameter totals.

|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Vanilla temporal Transformer** | Treat each of 385 time positions as a token whose 56 anonymous channel values are projected to `d_model`; add temporal positional encodings and attend across time. This models temporal relations, not explicit electrode geometry. | Full 385-token attention is quadratic in time. Parameter count is configuration-dependent. It has little local-signal bias, so with only 144 reported participants the trial count must not be mistaken for a large independent sample. It still assumes consistent 56-column ordering. | The original Transformer is not EEG-specific. It is easy to define and reproduce once fixed, but gives the weakest EEG-specific justification. Generic attention is not a novelty claim. |
| **B. EEG-specific temporal Transformer** | Keep time as the sequence axis and channels as features; add temporal encoding and EEG-motivated projection/embedding. He et al. provide a same-cohort precedent. | Still has a 385-position attention path unless tokenized/downsampled. It may add useful temporal bias, but the published same-cohort high result is not a verified unseen-participant estimate. More faithful to prior work than A; less local-convolution bias than C. Details in the base paper are incomplete or internally inconsistent. |
| **C. EEG Conformer-style convolution + attention** | Use temporal convolution/tokenization for local ERP morphology, then attention over the shorter temporal sequence. Channel values remain features; any channel-mixing layer uses anonymous fixed-order columns, not scalp coordinates. | Convolution adds parameters but can reduce sequence length and attention cost. This is a plausible small-data inductive bias, but it does not guarantee less overfitting. More complex than B and requires a careful parameter budget. A new Conformer variant would not alone establish novelty. |

### Published findings versus this audit’s assessment

Published studies establish that temporal Transformers and convolution-attention hybrids have been used for EEG, and that EEGNet is a compact, established baseline. The same-cohort papers establish that Transformer and EEGNet-family work already exists. **They do not establish that one of A/B/C is superior under a verified participant-disjoint protocol for the local data.** That comparison has not been run.

## 7. Recommended primary architecture and justification

**Conditional recommendation if the Transformer objective is retained: C, a small EEG Conformer-style temporal model.** Use a convolutional temporal stem to encode local waveform structure and reduce the temporal token count, followed by a shallow temporal Transformer and a fixed, simple classifier head. Treat the 56 channels as anonymous features in their stored order; do not add a scalp montage, electrode positional embedding, topomap output, or spatial interpretation until the channel mapping is established. Do not add a spectral/TBR branch without a separate evidence-based research question.

Why this is the most defensible *candidate*:

- It combines local temporal filtering with longer-range temporal attention, a reasonable inductive bias for an ERP-like `(385, 56)` input.
- Temporal token reduction can reduce attention-map cost; this is an engineering property, **not evidence of better generalization**.
- It is more EEG-informed than a vanilla Transformer without importing the master guide’s unsupported spectral biomarkers, filter settings, or topographic claims.
- The literature offers a general EEG Conformer precedent, while the same-cohort EEG-Transformer gives a direct comparison/reference. Neither provides proof of superiority under the project’s target evaluation.

Why not A: its weak local EEG bias and full 385-step attention make it a less compelling starting hypothesis for a small participant cohort.

Why not B: it is the closest reproduction of He et al., but that architecture is already published on the same cohort and its participant-level performance is unknown. It remains a useful reproduction comparator if that is the research question.

**Required baseline:** compact EEGNet, with ShallowConvNet as a second paper-family comparator if scope allows. Use identical verified participant partitions, data contract, metric, and selection budget. A dummy/majority baseline is a sanity check, not a substantive comparator. Do not claim C is better without matched experiments.

**Important conflict:** The current [Research Decision and Implementation Plan](docs/RESEARCH_DECISION_AND_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) explicitly selects EEGNet as the primary model and rejects a Transformer for its proposed reproducibility paper. This audit’s C recommendation is conditional on the user’s newer Transformer objective; it does not silently supersede or reconcile that project decision.
