# ADHD EEG Dataset Evaluation

##### [**Undermind**](https://undermind.ai)

---

**Research Goal:** Conduct a critical literature investigation for an EEG-based ADHD classification project using a dataset reported to contain 144 children across healthy control (HC), ADD, and ADHD groups, with 33,902 trials. Prioritize studies using this exact dataset and its original documentation, and compare relevant research before and after the 2023 EEG-Transformer paper. Examine conventional machine-learning, CNN, EEGNet, and Transformer approaches, with particular attention to participant-independent evaluation, data leakage, preprocessing validity, and reproducibility. Identify research gaps only where they remain defensible in light of contradictory evidence. Return primary studies with DOI or stable links, compare methods and evaluation protocols, and distinguish verified full-text findings from claims that require full-text verification. Do not presume that another Transformer, explainability, or subject-wise evaluation alone is novel, and do not recommend an architecture before establishing a meaningful research question.

## Summary of Results

The central result on the reported 144-child, 33,902-trial corpus is \[3\]’s 98.23% three-class CNN–LSTM accuracy; its abstract reports five-fold cross-validation but does not establish participant-disjoint testing, so the score is not evidence of generalization to unseen children \[3, 5\].

#### Model comparisons

- The earlier event-related EEG study reports EEGNet accuracy up to 83%, but could not distinguish ADHD subtypes \[1\]. \[3\] reports a higher three-class result; \[2\] reports 95.85% accuracy and AUC 0.9926 for EEG-Transformer versus CNN baselines. These are not comparable until cohort identity, split unit, and metric aggregation are checked in full text.
- \[4\] reports 99.78% on a public three-class dataset, but the abstract does not establish that this is the identical cohort or disclose a participant-wise protocol. Novel architecture or higher accuracy alone is therefore not a defensible advance.
- No returned record confirms a conventional-ML baseline on this exact 144-child cohort; \[10\]’s 91% SVM result is corpus-ambiguous.

#### Evaluation priorities

Partition participants before epoching or any data-dependent preprocessing; fit feature selection and tuning within training folds; aggregate and report at participant level with participant-level uncertainty. Segment leakage can inflate translational EEG results, though evidence from other disorders does not prove leakage in these ADHD papers \[5, 13\]; feature selection outside cross-validation is a separate bias route \[25\]. The supplied records are abstract-level: verify original dataset documentation, class counts, trial-to-participant mapping, preprocessing, code, and stable DOI/repository links before claiming replication or leakage.

1\. · 100% match · 2019 · 15 cit/yr\
**Deep Learning Based on Event-Related EEG Differentiates Children with ADHD from Healthy Controls** ([link](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm8071055))\
Amirali Vahid, Annet Bluschke, V. Roessner, Sebastian Stober, and C. Beste\
*Journal of Clinical Medicine* · Jul 1, 2019 · 110 citations

> Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is one of the most prevalent neuropsychiatric disorders in childhood and adolescence and its diagnosis is based on clinical interviews, symptom questionnaires, and neuropsychological testing. Much research effort has been undertaken to evaluate the usefulness of neurophysiological (EEG) data to aid this diagnostic process. In the current study, we applied deep learning methods on event-related EEG data to examine whether it is possible to distinguish ADHD patients from healthy controls using purely neurophysiological measures. The same was done to distinguish between ADHD subtypes. The results show that the applied deep learning model (“EEGNet”) was able to distinguish between both ADHD subtypes and healthy controls with an accuracy of up to 83%. However, a significant fraction of individuals could not be classified correctly. It is shown that neurophysiological processes indicating attentional selection associated with superior parietal cortical areas were the most important for that. Using the applied deep learning method, it was not possible to distinguish ADHD subtypes from each other. This is the first study showing that deep learning methods applied to EEG data are able to dissociate between ADHD patients and healthy controls. The results show that the applied method reflects a promising means to support clinical diagnosis in ADHD. However, more work needs to be done to increase the reliability of the taken approach.
